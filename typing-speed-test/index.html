Global Typing Speed Standards

What Is a Good Typing Speed (WPM)?

Typing speed is measured in Words Per Minute (WPM), where one standardized word equals 5 keystrokes (including spaces). While the average adult types between 38 and 41 WPM, employers in data entry, administration, software engineering, and transcription look for higher Net WPM combined with 96%+ accuracy.

Skill Tier Net Speed (WPM) Characters / Min (CPM) Recommended Accuracy Typical Career / Use Case
Beginner 15 – 30 WPM 75 – 150 CPM 85% – 92% Students learning touch-typing home row keys
Average Adult 38 – 41 WPM 190 – 205 CPM 92% – 96% Everyday email, coursework, and general computer use
Good (Silver Tier) 45 – 60 WPM 225 – 300 CPM 96% – 98% Office administration, customer support, and clerical roles
Professional (Gold Tier) 65 – 75 WPM 325 – 375 CPM 98% – 99% Data entry specialists, programmers, copywriters, and executive assistants
Advanced (Platinum Tier) 80 – 120+ WPM 400 – 600+ CPM 99%+ Real-time court reporting, medical transcription, and emergency dispatch

Fun Fact: The fastest English typing speed record on a standard typewriter was set by Stella Pajunas in 1946 at 216 WPM.

Scoring Methodology

How M Typing Test Calculates Your WPM & Accuracy

Step 1 • Raw Speed

Gross WPM

Every 5 typed characters (including spaces) count as 1 word. Gross WPM measures how fast your fingers move before deducting errors:

Gross WPM = (Total Chars ÷ 5) ÷ Minutes
Step 2 • Certified Score

Net WPM

Net WPM reflects your true productive typing speed by subtracting your uncorrected character errors per minute from your Gross WPM:

Net WPM = Gross WPM − (Errors ÷ Minutes)
Step 3 • Precision

Accuracy Percentage

Accuracy is the percentage of characters typed correctly out of total characters typed. Score over 50% accuracy to unlock your certificate:

Accuracy = ((Chars − Typos) ÷ Chars) × 100
Touch-Typing Guide

5 Proven Tips to Double Your Typing Speed

1

Master the Home Row Position

Rest your left fingers on A, S, D, F and your right fingers on J, K, L, ; with your thumbs lightly touching the Spacebar. Feel the tactile bumps on the F and J keys without looking down.

2

Prioritize Accuracy Over Raw Speed

Every typo penalizes your Net WPM and breaks muscle-memory rhythm. Aim for a steady 97%–99% accuracy on the Easy (180s) level before challenging the Hard (60s) technical passage.

3

Look One Word Ahead

Keep your eyes fixed on the screen and read 1 to 2 words ahead of the blue underlined cursor. Feeding upcoming words to your brain early eliminates hesitation between spaces.

4

Maintain Ergonomic Posture

Sit upright with elbows bent at a 90-degree angle, wrists floating slightly neutral above the desk, and the top of your monitor at eye level to prevent wrist strain during long sessions.

5

Practice Consistently for 15–30 Minutes a Day

Short, daily timed tests build neural pathways much faster than occasional marathon sessions. Start with a 3-minute Easy warm-up, take a 2-minute Medium test, and finish with a 1-minute Hard sprint to track your daily WPM gains and earn updated certificates.

How is WPM (Words Per Minute) calculated? +

Following the international typing standard, one word is standardized as 5 typed characters including spaces. Gross WPM divides your total typed characters by 5 and divides that figure by the elapsed time in minutes. Net WPM deducts your uncorrected errors per minute from your Gross WPM.

What is a good typing speed in WPM? +

The average adult typing speed is between 38 and 41 WPM. A score of 45 to 60 WPM is considered good for general office and administrative roles, 65 to 75 WPM is expected for professional typists and data entry specialists, and 80+ WPM is considered advanced.
